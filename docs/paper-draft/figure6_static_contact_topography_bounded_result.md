## One-sentence argument

Repeated interictal events delineated a patient-specific contact topography
that showed orientation-free spatial correspondence with early-ictal
broadband energy. Event order provided held-out information over the latest
two to three rank sets, but full-history recurrence did not improve over
history three or provide a cross-state increment over strong non-recurrent
controls.

## Methods

### Self-supervised modelling of interictal contact-rank events

We assessed whether within-event contact order contributed information beyond
patient-specific contact occurrence. Masked contact-rank events from 34
patients were ordered chronologically and divided within patient into 80%
training and 20% held-out partitions. A full-history gated recurrent unit
(GRU) predicted the next rank-tied contact set and subsequent contact
participation and rank distributions. Each event reset the recurrent state;
the model therefore represented rank history within individual interictal
events rather than slow state changes across events or hours. Three
prespecified seeds were used.

The full-history GRU was compared with a GRU trained after shuffling rank order
while preserving event membership, a no-history contact-hazard model, an
unordered-prefix model and a first-order model that received only the most
recent rank set. We distinguished two order tests. The formal test compared
held-out negative log-likelihood (NLL) between separately trained full and
rank-shuffle GRUs. A matched perturbation diagnostic instead shuffled order
when evaluating the already trained full GRU and quantified how strongly that
model used order.

We additionally fitted architecture-matched finite-history GRUs that replayed
only the latest one, two or three observed rank sets before each prediction.
The complete observed prefix continued to define the candidate-contact mask,
so contacts recruited before the finite window could not be selected again.
A matched history-three rank-shuffle model preserved event membership and
model capacity while disrupting recruitment order. This target-blind analysis
was performed in all 34 patients without accessing early-ictal values.

### Target-free estimation of interictal contact topography

For cross-state analysis, the contact field was fixed to interictal
participation. The simplest estimator was each contact's participation
frequency in the chronological training partition. Regularized
non-recurrent estimators comprised beta-binomial shrinkage, within-shaft and
geometry-based Laplacian smoothing, a Dirichlet contact-by-rank model and a
rank-1–4 low-rank logit estimator. Hyperparameters and the best regularized
estimator were selected by participation Brier score on a chronological
train60/validation20 subdivision of the train80 partition and were refitted
on train80. No early-ictal values were accessed during this selection.

The free-running GRU field was the cumulative contact participation
distribution generated by the frozen recurrent model. A teacher-forced field
was independently formed by aggregating one-step contact hazards along the
observed held-out prefixes. This comparison separated the field produced by
free rollout from contact probabilities evaluated along observed event
histories.

### Early-ictal target and fixed spatial readouts

The cross-state cohort comprised 16 patients with 106 seizures anchored to
clinical onset. For each seizure, the target was baseline-normalized
contact-level energy from 0 to 10 s after clinical onset in the 1–150 Hz band.
The same contact field was evaluated against every eligible seizure from a
patient; contact-wise Spearman correlations were first calculated within
seizure and then summarized by their patient-level median.

The fixed-direction endpoint tested whether greater interictal participation
corresponded to greater early-ictal energy. Orientation-free correspondence
was quantified by the absolute Spearman correlation and tested whether the
two states varied along the same patient-specific contact organization,
allowing either similar or reversed ordering. The orientation-free endpoint
does not test positive field replay or preservation of contact weights.

### Spatial nulls, confound sensitivity and statistics

All-contact permutations provided the direct continuation of the empirical
analysis. Stronger spatial sensitivities used circular and dihedral
permutations within electrode shafts and smooth random fields over the
patient's three-dimensional contact geometry. An equal-size shaft-profile
permutation was reported only where exchangeable shaft profiles existed.

One-confound-at-a-time partial-rank analyses separately adjusted for
within-shaft position, contact geometry, SOZ membership, pre-onset baseline
band power, 1–250 Hz broadband power and raw interictal participation.
Residual permutation was performed within patient. These tests were
sensitivities rather than a multivariable causal adjustment; grey/white-matter
labels and artifact-rejection rates were unavailable and were not imputed.

Patients were the independent statistical units. Seed-level outputs were
collapsed within patient before cohort inference. One-sided Wilcoxon tests
used exact signed-rank enumeration for the present sample sizes, with
Benjamini–Hochberg correction within prespecified comparison families.
Bootstrap intervals resampled patients.

## Results

### Event order carried held-out information, but full history was not optimal

The full GRU was sensitive to the order presented to it. Shuffling event order
at evaluation increased NLL in 32 of 34 patients (median NLL cost, 0.0100;
\(P=1.79\times10^{-8}\)). Separately training on true order also improved
held-out NLL over a GRU trained on within-event rank-shuffled events (median
gain, 0.0408; 27 of 34 patients positive; one-sided \(P=2.51\times10^{-4}\)).
However, the full-history GRU did not outperform the strongest nonrecurrent
prefix model (median gain, 0.0010; 17 of 34 patients positive;
\(P=0.440\)). Thus, event order carried generalizable information, but this
result did not establish that an unbounded recurrent history was necessary.

The finite-history comparison localized the useful information to recent
steps. History two improved over history one (median NLL gain, 0.0172; 32 of
34 patients positive; \(P=1.01\times10^{-6}\)), and history three improved
over history two (median gain, 0.0113; 29 of 34;
\(P=2.95\times10^{-8}\)). Full history did not improve over history three
(median gain, -0.0010; \(P=0.436\)). The ordered history-three model also
outperformed its architecture-matched rank-shuffle control (median gain,
0.0261; 27 of 34; \(P=0.00361\)). The accepted sequence result was therefore
bounded order dependence over the latest two to three rank sets, rather than
an unbounded recurrent state.

### Interictal and early-ictal fields did not share a fixed positive direction

The prespecified positive signed correspondence was heterogeneous across
patients. For the full-GRU participation field, the all-contact signed margin
had a median of 0.243, with positive values in 11 of 16 patients
(\(P=0.126\)). Signed margins also failed to reach cohort-level support under
within-shaft circular, within-shaft dihedral and geometry-smooth nulls. The
data therefore did not support the claim that contacts with greater
interictal participation consistently expressed greater early-ictal energy.

### Orientation-free contact organization was expressed across states

The orientation-free correspondence exceeded the all-contact null for the
full-GRU participation field (median \(|\rho|\) margin, 0.215; 14 of 16
patients positive; \(P=1.53\times10^{-4}\)). The correspondence persisted
under within-shaft circular permutations (median margin, 0.136; 13 of 16;
\(P=0.00205\)) and in the geometry-complete subset under smooth-field nulls
(median margin, 0.183; 9 of 13; \(P=0.00671\)). This result indicates that
interictal participation and early-ictal energy varied along a common
patient-specific spatial organization, but it permits either concordant or
reversed ordering across states.

### Recurrent sequence modelling did not provide a detectable static increment

Raw train80 participation showed a comparable orientation-free
correspondence (all-contact median margin, 0.196; 13 of 16;
\(P=6.56\times10^{-4}\)). Its effect also persisted under within-shaft and
geometry-smooth nulls. The full-GRU and best target-free regularized fields
were highly similar across contacts (median Spearman \(\rho=0.941\)). The
full-GRU advantage over the best regularized estimator was small and not
detected at the patient level (median margin difference, 0.0119;
\(P=0.108\)); the difference from the rank-shuffle GRU was also unsupported
(median, -0.00294; \(P=0.360\)).

Free rollout produced greater orientation-free correspondence than the
teacher-forced full-GRU field (median difference, 0.129;
\(P=0.00101\)). However, the teacher-forced full and rank-shuffle fields were
similar, and the free full-GRU field did not outperform raw or regularized
static participation. The free-versus-teacher difference therefore
characterized the effect of rollout but did not establish an
ordered-recurrent mechanism.

### Contact-level confounds did not explain the static correspondence

Orientation-free residual correspondence remained after separate adjustment
for shaft position, geometry, SOZ membership and baseline power. After
adjustment for pre-onset baseline band power, the full-GRU and raw fields had
nearly identical median residual margins (0.281 and 0.278, respectively; 15
of 16 patients positive for each). The full-GRU residual did not exceed the
rank-shuffle GRU (median difference, 0.0025; \(P=0.188\)) or the best
regularized estimator (0.0065; \(P=0.161\)). A small full-GRU increment was
observed only in the shaft-position sensitivity and did not replicate across
geometry, SOZ or power adjustments. These analyses reduce several
contact-level alternatives but do not constitute multivariable causal
control.

## Discussion

The computational analysis separates patient-specific spatial organization
from recurrent sequence dynamics. Repeated interictal events provided a stable
contact topography that was also expressed in early-ictal broadband energy,
but the direction of that relationship varied across patients. This pattern
is compatible with a common spatial substrate that is reweighted across
brain states rather than preservation of a fixed signed contact ordering
across states.

The sequence models set a boundary on this interpretation. The full GRU
responded to event order, and a model trained on true order outperformed one
trained on rank-shuffled events. This information was concentrated in the
latest two to three rank sets: full history did not improve over history three
or the strongest nonrecurrent prefix model. The cross-state correspondence
was also already present in raw and regularized participation fields.
Unbounded recurrent hidden states, physical axes and source directions are
therefore not required by the current result. This does not exclude a
patient-specific pathological axis; it shows that the present contact-rank
data and model comparisons do not identify it uniquely.

The early-ictal analysis used a target that had been examined during several
stages of model review. It should consequently be treated as internal
same-dataset validation rather than independent confirmation. External
replication requires previously unexamined patients with exact clinical-onset
anchors and the same frozen target and spatial-null definitions.

## Claim boundary

### Supported

- Within-event rank order affected the computation of the trained GRU.
- True event order improved held-out prediction over rank-shuffle training.
- The useful order-dependent information was concentrated in the latest two
  to three rank sets.
- Interictal participation and early-ictal energy showed orientation-free
  patient-specific spatial correspondence in the current dataset.
- The correspondence persisted under within-shaft and geometry-aware nulls
  and after several one-confound-at-a-time adjustments.

### Not established

- Full-history recurrence improved held-out prediction over the strongest
  nonrecurrent prefix model.
- Interictal participation and early-ictal energy shared a fixed positive
  direction.
- The cross-state correspondence required recurrent sequence modelling.
- The GRU recovered a pathological axis, source or biological latent state.
- Interictal trajectories were replayed during seizures.
- The current finding generalizes to independent patients.

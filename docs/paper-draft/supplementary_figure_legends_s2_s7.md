# Supplementary figure legends S2–S7

**Supplementary Fig. 2 | SOZ localization of raw and group-event-filtered interictal HFO burden.** **A,** Subject-level receiver operating characteristic curves for distinguishing clinically labelled seizure onset zone (SOZ) from non-SOZ contacts using contact-wise group-event-filtered high-frequency oscillation (HFO) burden in the Yuquan (20 patients) and Epilepsiae (15 patients) cohorts; grey lines denote individual patients, coloured lines the cohort mean, shading the s.e.m., and inset points the patient-level area under the curve (AUC), with the black line indicating the mean. Mean AUC was 0.873 in Yuquan and 0.955 in Epilepsiae. **B,** Paired patient-level AUC before (Raw) and after restricting refined HFO detections to accepted population-event windows (Synchronized); bars and black horizontal lines indicate cohort means, and points joined by lines denote paired patients (Yuquan, 0.837 versus 0.873; Epilepsiae, 0.939 versus 0.955). Statistical significance was assessed using a two-sided paired Wilcoxon test; *P* < 0.05; n.s., *P* ≥ 0.05.

**Supplementary Fig. 3 | Model-order scanning identifies two temporal templates in most patients while retaining higher-order solutions.** **A,** Unsupervised clustering was evaluated for *K* = 2–10 using event-by-contact recruitment-rank features after masking non-participating contacts in 40 patients; for each patient, *K* was selected as the solution with the highest median silhouette among those with median adjusted mutual information (AMI) ≥ 0.70 across seeds and a smallest-cluster fraction ≥ 0.10. The left panel shows the selected-*K* distribution (34 of 40 patients selected *K* = 2), the middle panel shows each patient's silhouette difference relative to *K* = 2 with the cohort median and interquartile range, and the right panel shows the percentage of patients passing both gates at each *K*. **B,** Recruitment-order maps for two *K* = 4 solutions (E11 and E19) and one *K* = 6 solution (Y10), with 2,500 displayed population events per patient; colour denotes relative within-event recruitment order from first to last, light grey denotes a non-participating contact and hatched gaps separate templates. Profiles at right show the contact-wise mean rank for each template, with shading indicating ±1 s.d.

**Supplementary Fig. 4 | Three-dimensional direction shifts paired template axes and improves held-out directional organization.** **A,** Paired half-rose distributions of the directed angular separation between the full-fit TA and TB axes obtained using Timing-only (blue) and Timing + 3D direction (orange) clustering in the same 28 patients. Here, 0° denotes the same direction and 180° the opposite direction; overlapping bars show the fraction of patients in 30° bins, with the blended region denoting the shared fraction. Coloured diamonds mark means and outer arcs show mean ± 1 s.d. The mean separation was 101.3° ± 45.0° for Timing-only and 134.6° ± 34.4° for Timing + 3D direction. **B,** Paired patient-level direction scores for Timing-only and Timing + 3D direction clustering, evaluated by alternating-recording-block cross-validation. All interictal events contributed timing features, whereas events with finite direction estimates additionally contributed the masked spatial view. The score is the equal-fold, equal-template mean signed cosine between each held-out event direction and its assigned training-template axis. Points denote patients, lines preserve patient pairing, violins show the distributions, boxes show IQRs and black horizontal lines mark medians. The median score increased from 0.435 to 0.479; 22 of 26 patients improved, with a median paired gain of 0.042 (95% bootstrap CI, 0.011–0.064; one-sided paired Wilcoxon *P* = 9.54 × 10<sup>−6</sup>). **C,** The same held-out direction scores compared with the patient-specific median of each method's direction-shuffle null. Open and filled distributions denote null and observed scores, respectively; grey lines preserve within-patient pairing. Timing-only and Timing + 3D direction scores both exceeded their respective null medians in all 26 patients (one-sided paired Wilcoxon *P* = 1.49 × 10<sup>−8</sup> for each method). The upper bracket compares the two observed methods (one-sided paired Wilcoxon *P* = 9.54 × 10<sup>−6</sup>). Stars indicate statistical significance; exact *P* values are reported here. **D,** Clinical SOZ-contact compactness relative to each patient's mapped invasive-contact geometry. The SOZ root-mean-square (RMS) radius was divided by the median radius of 20,000 equally sized random contact subsets from the same patient; the dashed line marks the null ratio of 1. Points denote patients (blue, Yuquan, n = 15; purple, Epilepsiae, n = 14), filled points indicate a patient-level empirical *P* < 0.05, and horizontal and vertical black lines mark medians and IQRs. Brackets show one-sided within-dataset Wilcoxon signed-rank tests of log2 ratios against 0 (Yuquan, *P* = 3.1 × 10<sup>−5</sup>; Epilepsiae, *P* = 1.2 × 10<sup>−4</sup>; *** denotes *P* < 0.001). Across both cohorts, the median ratio was 0.375 (95% bootstrap CI, 0.261–0.478; n = 29 patients; one-sided Wilcoxon *P* = 3.7 × 10<sup>−9</sup>).

**Supplementary Fig. 5 | Multiband ictal concordance with Timing + 3D direction templates.** **A,** Patient-level field-concordance difference (Δ) across seven frequency bands in 17 patients. For each patient and band, *D* is the median early-ictal dense-grid spatial concordance with the better-matching TA or TB field defined by Timing + 3D direction clustering after resolving sign and transverse-mirror symmetry, and Δ is *D* minus the median of that patient's all-contact permutation null (1,000 draws); points denote patients, violins the distributions and black horizontal lines the cohort medians. Red violins and stars denote one-sided *P* < 0.05 against each band's own coherent cohort spatial null; these per-band tests are not corrected across the seven bands. **B,** Heat map of the same patient-by-band Δ values, with rows ordered by the number of bands exceeding the patient's own null and the bottom row showing cohort medians; outlined stars denote one-sided empirical patient-versus-own-null *P* < 0.05 and are not cohort-level or multiplicity-corrected. Bands are δ (1–4 Hz), θ (4–8 Hz), α (8–13 Hz), β (13–30 Hz), γ (30–80 Hz), R (80–150 Hz) and FR (150–250 Hz). The δ, θ, α, β and γ bands reached *P* < 0.05 in the per-band tests; no band passed the seven-band maxT correction (minimum *P*<sub>FWER</sub> = 0.058), and the direct between-band omnibus test was also not significant (calibrated *P* = 0.522).

**Supplementary Fig. 7 | Frozen local-connectivity ablation and model-event KMeans structure.** **A,** Mode 1 share among classified formal events, defined as Mode 1/(Mode 1 + Mode 2). **B,** Complementary Mode 2 share. **C,** Balanced alignment between de novo KMeans *K* = 2 clusters and the frozen Mode 1/2 labels. The natural clusters were not relabelled as patient modes. **D,** Fraction of returned events outside the frozen patient-distribution support (out of distribution, OOD). Across A–D, Node, +EE, +E-to-I and +EE+EI were evaluated for 20 s in each of 12 paired network seeds (1581–1592) under frozen topology, delays and separately conserved incoming-weight budgets. Open circles denote individual networks, grey lines connect the same network seed across arms, filled circles show equal-network means and error bars show 90% network-bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs). Stars indicate that the paired arm-minus-Node 90% network-bootstrap CI excluded zero (4,096 resamples; no multiplicity correction): +E-to-I in A and B, +EE+EI in C, and +E-to-I and +EE+EI in D. **E,** Masked recruitment-rank heatmap for 627 formal clean model events grouped by frozen MTA and MTB KMeans labels, with aligned per-contact rank distributions and one shared first-to-last colour scale. Mode 1/2 and MTA/MTB are frozen model labels and have no independent pathological interpretation. These analyses estimate model-internal pathway effects and event structure in the frozen E10 development case; they do not establish patient causal connectivity, anatomical-core recovery or patient-blind/real-geometry generalization.

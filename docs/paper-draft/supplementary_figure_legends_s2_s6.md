# Supplementary figure legends S2–S6

**Supplementary Fig. 2 | SOZ localization of raw and group-event-filtered interictal HFO burden.** **A,** Subject-level receiver operating characteristic curves for distinguishing clinically labelled seizure onset zone (SOZ) from non-SOZ contacts using contact-wise group-event-filtered high-frequency oscillation (HFO) burden in the Yuquan (20 patients) and Epilepsiae (15 patients) cohorts; grey lines denote individual patients, coloured lines the cohort mean, shading the s.e.m., and inset points the patient-level area under the curve (AUC), with the black line indicating the mean. Mean AUC was 0.873 in Yuquan and 0.955 in Epilepsiae. **B,** Paired patient-level AUC before (Raw) and after restricting refined HFO detections to accepted population-event windows (Synchronized); bars and black horizontal lines indicate cohort means, and points joined by lines denote paired patients (Yuquan, 0.837 versus 0.873; Epilepsiae, 0.939 versus 0.955). Statistical significance was assessed using a two-sided paired Wilcoxon test; *P* < 0.05; n.s., *P* ≥ 0.05.

**Supplementary Fig. 3 | Model-order scanning identifies two temporal templates in most patients while retaining higher-order solutions.** **A,** Unsupervised clustering was evaluated for *K* = 2–10 using event-by-contact recruitment-rank features after masking non-participating contacts in 40 patients; for each patient, *K* was selected as the solution with the highest median silhouette among those with median adjusted mutual information (AMI) ≥ 0.70 across seeds and a smallest-cluster fraction ≥ 0.10. The left panel shows the selected-*K* distribution (34 of 40 patients selected *K* = 2), the middle panel shows each patient's silhouette difference relative to *K* = 2 with the cohort median and interquartile range, and the right panel shows the percentage of patients passing both gates at each *K*. **B,** Recruitment-order maps for two *K* = 4 solutions (E11 and E19) and one *K* = 6 solution (Y10), with 2,500 displayed population events per patient; colour denotes relative within-event recruitment order from first to last, light grey denotes a non-participating contact and hatched gaps separate templates. Profiles at right show the contact-wise mean rank for each template, with shading indicating ±1 s.d.

**Supplementary Fig. 4 | Direction and held-out spatial read-back of interictal recruitment axes.** **A,** Half-rose distribution of the directed angular separation between the spatial axes associated with paired interictal temporal templates TA and TB across 28 patients, where 0° denotes the same direction and 180° the opposite direction; sector labels give patient counts in 30° bins, the radial black line marks the median and the outer black arc the interquartile range (IQR). **B,** Patient-level Spearman correlation between held-out contact recruitment rank and the path-axis coordinate fitted using the complementary event half in Yuquan (n = 7 patients) and Epilepsiae (n = 19 patients). Points denote patients (blue, Yuquan; purple, Epilepsiae), violins show the distributions, horizontal black lines mark medians and vertical black lines mark IQRs. Across both cohorts, the median held-out ρ was 0.752 (IQR, 0.500–0.842; n = 26 patients).

**Supplementary Fig. 5 | Early ictal spectral phenotypes and a gamma-dominant example.** **A,** Mutually exclusive early ictal spectral phenotypes across 324 seizures from 23 patients: broadband 1–150 Hz (124, 38.3%), gamma-dominant 30–80 Hz (76, 23.5%), low-frequency only 1–13 Hz (7, 2.2%) and other patterns (117, 36.1%). **B,** Representative gamma-dominant seizure (E20, seizure 7), showing common-average-referenced SEEG traces, the baseline-normalized time–frequency representation from contact HRB1 and band-power trajectories for 1–30, 30–80, 80–150 and 1–150 Hz. Time is aligned to clinical onset at 0 s; blue shading marks the −120 to −90 s baseline and red shading the 0–10 s early clinical window. This single-seizure panel illustrates the signal morphology of the gamma-dominant category and was not used for cohort-level inference.

**Supplementary Fig. 6 | Multiband sensitivity analysis of ictal-to-interictal field concordance.** **A,** Patient-level field-concordance difference (Δ) across seven frequency bands in 17 patients. For each patient and band, *D* is the median early-ictal dense-grid spatial concordance with the better-matching of two predefined interictal template fields after resolving sign and transverse-mirror symmetry, and Δ is *D* minus the median of that patient's all-contact permutation null (1,000 draws); points denote patients, violins the distributions and black horizontal lines the cohort medians. Red violins and stars denote bands significant under a coherent seven-band maxT family-wise error-rate correction (*P*<sub>FWER</sub> < 0.05). **B,** Heat map of the same patient-by-band Δ values, with rows ordered by the number of bands exceeding the patient's own null and the bottom row showing cohort medians; outlined stars denote one-sided empirical patient-versus-own-null *P* < 0.05 and are not cohort-level or multiplicity-corrected. Bands are δ (1–4 Hz), θ (4–8 Hz), α (8–13 Hz), β (13–30 Hz), γ (30–80 Hz), R (80–150 Hz) and FR (150–250 Hz).
